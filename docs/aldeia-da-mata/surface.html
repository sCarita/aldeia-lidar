Trees across Aldeia da Mata

Trees covers the whole 37.28 km² parish. A lightweight overview shows one measured treetop for each of 250,323 estimated crowns. Zoom below a 1,500 m view span to load detailed 1 m canopy cells and measured 3D vegetation points within a 750 m square around the camera target. Nine nearby 250 m tiles are requested at most, with two downloads at once and a twelve-tile cache. Loading pauses while you navigate. Canopy cells use the highest supported vegetation return, measured above the existing 0.5 m ground model. Full source hierarchy levels yielded 165,576,602 normalized vegetation returns, 14,623,653 occupied canopy cells and 7,323,951 display points.

Tree clusters colours globally segmented crowns consistently across all tiles. The parish is segmented before display tiles are cut, so crossing a tile border does not create another tree. IDs belong to this parish dataset and differ from the earlier village-only IDs. Tap a crown or treetop for its maximum measured height, occupied-cell area and median NDVI proxy. Crown counts and combined filters refer to the whole parish, while only nearby detailed points are loaded. Adjoining trees may merge or split and understory trees concealed below taller crowns may be missed. Blank canopy cells do not establish bare ground. Parish vegetation processing, source ranges and limitations.

Show or hide vegetation

Turn off Vegetation in the explorer heading to hide all tree and canopy points while keeping the terrain and the other layer controls active. This works in both map and 3D, including the wide crown overview. Tree selection is cleared, vegetation detail loading pauses, and tree filters are disabled while hidden. Turning Vegetation on restores your height, NDVI and clustering settings.

Buildings alongside trees

The Buildings toggle adds measured DGT class 6 returns in blue-grey in both the map and 3D. Roof maps retain the highest measured point per occupied 0.5 m cell; 3D retains one measured point per 1 m XYZ voxel. A 4 m roof overview supplies wider views, with nearby 250 m tiles loading as you zoom. Buildings retain absolute source elevations independently of gaps in the ground model. Tree height, NDVI filters and crown selection apply only to vegetation. These are classified structure points, not cadastral footprints, a complete building inventory or solid models. Hidden walls and unclassified structures may be absent. Building processing and source ranges.

Bridge decks

The Bridges checkbox adds DGT class 26 bridge-deck returns in amber, independently of Buildings and tree filters. Use the adjacent ↗ button to focus an area with classified bridge points, then switch to 3D for their measured elevation. Surface cells retain the highest measured return at 0.5 m spacing; 3D uses 1 m XYZ voxel representatives and the overview uses 4 m cells. No ground mask, interpolation, invented supports or clearance estimate is applied. This source class may omit culverts and unclassified crossings and is not a complete bridge inventory. Bridge processing and source ranges.

Water returns

The Water toggle shows measured DGT class 9 points in cyan in map and 3D, independently of tree filters and the other layers. The adjacent ↗ button focuses a tile with water returns. Highest measured 0.5 m surface cells, 1 m XYZ voxel representatives and a 4 m overview keep navigation light. Coordinates and source elevations are retained; no shorelines, continuous water planes or depths are invented. These survey-date returns do not establish current water extent, depth, volume, flow or quality. Sparse or missing returns do not establish dry land. Water source and processing details.

Other / unclassified points

The Unclassified toggle adds DGT class 1 returns in lilac in map and 3D, independently of the tree filters and other layers. The adjacent ↗ button focuses a tile with these points. Source elevations are retained without a ground-support mask or height filtering. The map retains the highest point per occupied 0.5 m cell; 3D uses measured 1 m XYZ voxel representatives, with a 4 m overview for wider views. This is a mixed source category, not an identification of particular objects or a layer of archaeological features. Class 0 is not included; it was absent in the full project census. Unclassified source and processing details.

Exploratory NDVI proxy

Choose NDVI proxy in the vegetation colour selector to inspect (NIR − red) / (NIR + red), using the DGT public viewer’s 8-bit colour channels. Reflectance calibration and exact photography dates have not been verified. The source filenames contain 04-2024. This colour ratio is an exploratory proxy and does not establish tree health or support calibrated NDVI thresholds. Grey points have zero, saturated or very dark band values that are masked. At detailed views, tap a canopy cell for its ratio or enable Tree clusters for a median across valid occupied canopy cells in that estimated crown. The wide overview uses crown medians. Crown summaries include their valid/total cell count. Filter NDVI proxy applies an inclusive minimum and maximum alongside the height filter. With Tree clusters enabled, a crown’s median determines whether that whole crown passes; otherwise each vegetation sample is filtered by its own value. Missing values are excluded when the filter is active. The filter also works with height colours. Channel checks, source files, masks and limitations.

From the parish to local detail

The parish surface is clipped to the official CAOP2025 boundary. A 4 m base grid provides context. As you zoom in, compressed 250 m terrain tiles supply a 0.5 m grid around the camera target. Mesh spacing adapts from 2 m to 1 m and reaches 0.5 m below a 200 m view span. Maximum detail mode also loads a focus window at wider views.

How this surface was made

The maximum-detail tiles use every available point-cloud hierarchy level, including the deepest levels, across all 60 source tiles. Ground returns are averaged into 0.5 m cells with 0.25 m Gaussian smoothing. Gaps more than 5 m from measured ground are blank. Terrain tiles load locally as needed and use the official parish boundary. The 0.5 m grid is the finest prepared surface in this viewer.

Full-detail source and processing metadata

The parish surface averages ground returns from DGT viewer hierarchy levels 1–3 into 4 m cells, fills empty cells from the nearest measurement, smooths with a 2 m Gaussian sigma and omits cells farther than 16 m from measurements. The boundary mask follows CAOP2025. The village and Tapadão patches use more detailed chunks, 0.5 m cells, 0.5 m smoothing and a 5 m gap limit. Shading uses each surface’s grid.

These are locally derived terrain models. The cell sizes describe the output grids. Local detail also depends on the spacing and classification of the underlying measurements. Height exaggeration changes the display.
